Research Question: Do ski resorts' posted snowfall amounts match independently tracked snowfall amounts? 
I am taking inspiration from an older study that I recently read about that did similar research about snowfall totals. Here is a link to that study: https://aeastaging.aeaweb.org/research/when-do-ski-resorts-exaggerate-new-snowfall
Who would care about this question: The ski community would be a group that cares about this question. This is important because if the data is inflated or skewed, it can change your ski day as well as your plans. 
What do I expect: I expect to see a slight difference between the total snowfall that is reported by mountains and the total snowfall that is tracked but independent research. I would be surprised to see no difference in the totals because I could see how resorts inflate their totals in order to influence more people to come skiing. I would also be surprised to see a large and noticeable difference in total snowfall. 

Data Source and Methods: 
I will be comparing posted snowfall totals for 2025, a notably poor snow year, and comparing three Colorado ski resorts to three corresponding locations with SNOTEL stations. The three locations that I will be studying are Vail Mountain, Copper Mountain, and Loveland. It is also important to note that I chose these three specific mountains because they operate under 3 different parent companies. (Vail-Epic Pass, Copper-Ikon Pass, Loveland-Indy Pass) I will be using an API to track SNOTEL data, and using the wayback machine archive to track resorts reported data. I am using the Wayback machine to track resorts rather than scraping because the terms and services most likely will limit or fully restrict this type of action.  
Sources: 
Wayback Machine: https://web.archive.org/web/20251015121804/https://www.vail.com/
SNOTEL Data API https://wcc.sc.egov.usda.gov/awdbRestApi/swagger-ui/index.html#/Data/getData


Scope of the Project: I will be following three different resorts and their paired SNOTEL data from December 1, 2025 to April 1, 2026. This will give me roughly 120 days of snowfall data to look at. I am also planning on looking into the weekend report data because this is an area where I am predicting there to be a greater difference in reported snowfall as resorts prepare for a bigger rush of skiers and snowboarders. 
Analysis: The majority of my analysis will come from the comparison of snowfall totals. I will use the difference in the reported amounts by each group in order to find the total difference in reported snowfall. I will also use visualizations to show these differences over time to see if different days or periods of time yield different reported totals.
